Governance Framework

📌 Official Auditable Requirement Statement

The organization shall establish and maintain a formal governance framework for the information security and privacy program that defines the structure, processes, and practices through which security and privacy decisions are made, implemented, and overseen. The framework shall articulate the organization's approach to governing security and privacy risks and shall be aligned with the organization's overall governance structure. The framework shall provide the foundation for all security and privacy policies, standards, and procedures and shall be reviewed periodically to ensure it remains appropriate for the organization's risk environment, regulatory obligations, and strategic objectives.

📌 Requirement

Official Auditable Requirement Statement

The organization shall establish and maintain a formal governance framework for the information security and privacy program that defines the structure, processes, and practices through which security and privacy decisions are made, implemented, and overseen. The framework shall articulate the organization's approach to governing security and privacy risks and shall be aligned with the organization's overall governance structure. The framework shall provide the foundation for all security and privacy policies, standards, and procedures and shall be reviewed periodically to ensure it remains appropriate for the organization's risk environment, regulatory obligations, and strategic objectives.

💡 Why this Requirement Exists

To establish a consistent governance foundation for information security and privacy, ensuring decisions, oversight, policies, and responsibilities operate within a defined and accountable framework.

🛠️ Implementation Guidance

Define the governance structure, roles, committees, decision rights, and processes; align the framework with enterprise governance and risk management; document supporting policies and procedures; obtain leadership approval; review the framework periodically.

💼 Business Examples

A financial services organization establishes an enterprise security and privacy governance framework linking board oversight, the CISO, risk committees, business units, and control owners, with defined decision and escalation processes.

⚠️ Common Audit Findings

No formal governance framework; governance roles or decision rights unclear; framework not aligned with enterprise governance; supporting processes undocumented; framework not reviewed or updated.

🚫 Common Mistakes

Creating a framework that is too high-level; failing to define decision rights; treating security governance as separate from enterprise governance; not documenting supporting processes; allowing the framework to become outdated.

⭐ Best Practices

Align security and privacy governance with enterprise governance; define clear roles and escalation paths; document decision rights; maintain supporting policies and procedures; review the framework regularly and after significant changes.

🛡️ Parent Control Overview

📌 GOV-001: Information Security Governance

Establishes the foundational governance structures, accountabilities, and decision-making processes for the enterprise information security program, ensuring alignment with business strategy and regulatory requirements.

📝 Business Description

Establish, govern, operate, monitor, and continually improve an enterprise information security program that manages information security, cybersecurity, privacy, resilience, and regulatory compliance risks.

🎯 Business Objectives & Risk Mitigations

🎯 Business Objective

To ensure a structured and accountable approach to managing security and compliance across the organization.

🛡️ Business Benefits

Aligns security with business strategy; provides clear accountability; enables informed executive decisions; reduces regulatory exposure; optimizes security investments; builds stakeholder confidence.

⚠️ Business Risks If Missing

Unclear accountability; misaligned security investments; regulatory non-compliance; ineffective decision-making; fragmented security program; increased risk exposure; loss of stakeholder trust.
